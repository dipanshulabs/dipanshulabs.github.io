Time of Concentration Calculator

Calculate catchment time of concentration (T_c) using three standard empirical methods. T_c is the critical input for Rational Method peak discharge and design storm duration selection.

Kirpich Method — Inputs

Best for small natural and agricultural catchments with a well-defined channel. Developed from data on 7 catchments in Tennessee, USA (Kirpich, 1940).

m
Longest flow path from catchment divide to outlet
m
Height difference between highest catchment point and outlet

FAA Method — Inputs

Developed by the US Federal Aviation Administration for airport drainage. Widely adopted for urban and paved catchments because it incorporates the runoff coefficient C.

m
Length of the overland (sheet) flow path to the channel
Same C as used in Rational Method Q = CiA · Paved: 0.85–0.95 · Suburban: 0.35–0.55 · Grass: 0.20–0.35
%
Average slope of the overland flow surface in percent (%)

Bransby-Williams — Inputs

Suitable for natural rural and semi-arid catchments. Accounts for both stream length and catchment area. Widely used in Middle East, Africa and South Asian drainage practice.

m
Length of the longest watercourse from divide to outlet (m)
km²
m/km
Average slope of the main stream channel in metres per km. Example: 10 m rise over 5 km = 2.0 m/km
Result — Time of Concentration

Select a method and press Calculate.

Kirpich Formula (1940)

T_c = 0.0195 × L^0.77 × S^−0.385
T_c = time of concentration (minutes) L = hydraulic length of catchment (m) S = slope = H / L (m/m, dimensionless) H = elevation difference (m)

FAA Formula

T_c = 1.8 × (1.1 − C) × L^0.5 / S^(1/3)
T_c = time of concentration (minutes) C = runoff coefficient (dimensionless, 0–1) L = overland flow length (m) S = average surface slope (%)

Bransby-Williams Formula

T_c = 0.057 × L / (A^0.1 × S^0.2)
T_c = time of concentration (minutes) L = main stream length (m) A = catchment area (km²) S = average stream slope (m/km)
T_c defines the critical storm duration for the Rational Method. When calculated T_c is less than the minimum storm duration in your IDF data, use the minimum IDF duration instead. Always use the method most appropriate for your catchment type — see the guidance section below.
Method Selection Guide

When to use which method

No single method is universally correct. Your choice depends on catchment size, land use, data availability and regional practice. The table and cards below guide the selection.

Rural / Natural

Kirpich Method

  • Small natural or agricultural catchments
  • Well-defined single channel flow path
  • Only two inputs needed (L and H)
  • Quick preliminary estimate
  • Steep to moderately sloped catchments
  • Not suitable for urban / paved areas
  • Less reliable for very large catchments
  • Tends to underestimate T_c for flat terrain
Typical range: 1 ha – 50 km² · Slope > 0.5%
Urban / Paved

FAA Method

  • Urban, suburban and paved catchments
  • Airport and road drainage design
  • Overland (sheet) flow dominated paths
  • Accounts for surface type via C coefficient
  • Well suited to small urban sub-catchments
  • Commonly accepted by Middle East authorities
  • Not appropriate for natural catchments
  • Less reliable for long channel flow paths
Typical range: 0.5 ha – 5 km² · Urban areas
Rural / Semi-Arid

Bransby-Williams

  • Medium to large natural catchments
  • Semi-arid and arid regions (Middle East, Africa)
  • Accounts for catchment area (unlike Kirpich)
  • Suitable for wadi and dryland hydrology
  • Widely accepted in Gulf region drainage standards
  • Good for catchments with defined stream network
  • Not intended for urban catchments
  • Requires area in km² — less useful for tiny catchments
Typical range: 1 km² – 1,000 km² · Arid / rural

Professional tip: For important projects, calculate T_c using two or more methods and compare results. If values differ significantly, review your input data and select the method most appropriate to your catchment characteristics. For urban masterplan drainage design in the Middle East, the FAA method is most commonly accepted by authorities. For wadi or rural catchment modelling, Bransby-Williams is the preferred choice. Always confirm the acceptable method with the relevant authority before submission.
