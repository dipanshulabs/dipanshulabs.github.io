Hazen-Williams Formula

V = 0.849 × C × R^0.63 × S^0.54
Q = V × A = V × π(D/2)²
V = mean velocity (m/s) C = Hazen-Williams coefficient (dimensionless) R = hydraulic radius = D/4 for full circular pipe (m) S = hydraulic gradient = h_f / L (m/m) Q = flow rate (m³/s) A = pipe area = π(D/2)² (m²)
Hazen-Williams is empirical and best suited to water at normal temperatures in full turbulent pipe flow. For precise work, highly viscous fluids, or small-diameter pipes, use Darcy-Weisbach instead.

Darcy-Weisbach Formula

h_f = f × (L / D) × (V² / 2g)
h_f = friction headloss (m) f = Darcy friction factor (dimensionless) L = pipe length (m) D = internal pipe diameter (m) V = mean flow velocity (m/s) g = 9.81 m/s²

Colebrook-White (friction factor)

1/√f = −2 log₁₀(ε/3.7D + 2.51/Re√f)
ε = pipe roughness height (m) Re = Reynolds number = V × D / ν Solved iteratively — converges in <20 steps Laminar (Re < 2000): f = 64 / Re
Darcy-Weisbach is theoretically rigorous and valid for all fluids, temperatures, and pipe sizes. Preferred for precise engineering design and where conditions vary from standard water.
