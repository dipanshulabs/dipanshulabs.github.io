Hazen-Williams Formula

V = 0.849 × C × R^0.63 × S^0.54
Q = V × A = V × π(D/2)²
V = mean velocity (m/s) C = Hazen-Williams coefficient R = hydraulic radius = D/4 for full circular pipe (m) S = hydraulic gradient = h_f / L (m/m) Q = flow rate (m³/s) A = pipe cross-section area = π(D/2)² (m²)
Hazen-Williams is empirical and valid for water at normal temperatures in full pipe flow. For highly viscous fluids, very small pipes, or laminar flow conditions, use Darcy-Weisbach.

Darcy-Weisbach Formula

h_f = f × (L / D) × (V² / 2g)
h_f = friction headloss (m) f = Darcy friction factor (Colebrook-White, dimensionless) L = pipe length (m) D = internal diameter (m) V = mean velocity (m/s) g = 9.81 m/s²

Colebrook-White (friction factor)

1/√f = −2 log₁₀(ε/3.7D + 2.51/Re√f)
ε = roughness height (m) Re = V × D / ν (Reynolds number) Solved iteratively — converges in < 20 iterations Laminar (Re < 2000): f = 64 / Re
Darcy-Weisbach is theoretically rigorous and works for all fluids, temperatures, and pipe sizes. Preferred over Hazen-Williams for design work requiring accuracy across varying conditions.
